# CLAUDE.md — Project rules and approved decisions

@PROJECT_INSTRUCTIONS.md

The full development rules are in `PROJECT_INSTRUCTIONS.md` (copy of the Claude Project instructions, imported above).
Gameplay intent lives in `GAME_DESIGN.md`. This file records decisions the owner has approved, which clarify or override those documents.

## Approved decisions (2026-10-28)

1. **Physics:** custom arcade driving model and custom collisions. Matter.js only if collisions prove inadequate in testing.
2. **Saving:** all persistence goes through `src/platform/PlatformManager.js` (platform storage on platforms; LocalStorage only as the local browser fallback). Gameplay code never touches LocalStorage directly.
3. **Size:** target **under 15 MB** (YouTube Playables recommended initial-bundle size); 30 MB is the absolute maximum. Measure every milestone.
4. **World:** one large bounded arena for the prototype; keep world code flexible for a larger or endless world later.
5. **Process:** one milestone at a time. Do not start the next milestone until the owner has tested and approved the current one.
6. **Owner is not a programmer:** explain decisions in plain language and give exact step-by-step instructions for anything they must do manually.
7. **Graphics:** everything is code-drawn low-poly (no image files); `ART_DIRECTION.md` is the style guide (still a draft).
8. **Milestone 1 (approved 2026-10-28):** temporary solid wall bounce at arena edges; capped skid marks; three temporary handling presets (Grippy / Balanced / Drifty); car drives on page load, R restarts.
9. **2.5D presentation (Milestone 1.5, approved 2026-10-28):** stylized 2.5D comic-book look, high-angle oblique camera (north-up, fixed, 55° default under test). Physics stays flat and never reads visual settings. All drawing goes through `src/render/projection.js`. Rules in `RENDERING.md`. No shaders.
10. **Low-poly 2.5D look (Milestone 1.6, approved with M1.7 on 2026-10-29):** game name "Can't Catch Me!". Minimalist low-poly, flat-shaded, matching the camera/geometry/lighting approach of the owner's PAKO Forever reference frames (not their assets). Fixed orthographic camera with the map turned (40°) and tilted (48°); cars and props built from 3D solids and drawn once at startup (cars in 64 directions). Replaces the north-up camera and comic outlines of decision 9. Draft style rules in `ART_DIRECTION.md`.
11. **Full game in one pass (2026-10-29):** owner said "proceed with the rest of the milestones and provide the full game" (M2–M5 together, then test). Single Shockwave power-up, no adverts, no analytics.
12. **Endless world (Step 2, 2026-10-30):** owner asked for an endless chunk-streaming world with sinkholes instead of a fence. Replaces decision 4. Active grid is 3 x 3 chunks; chunks are 2400 units, not the 1200 suggested as an example, because the grid only guarantees one chunk's width ahead and the camera shows about 2030 units on tall phones (`tests/test_world.mjs` checks this). Every chunk keeps the same 600-unit road grid, so roads always line up; holes never touch a chunk's border roads.
13. **Pursuit (Step 3, 2026-10-30):** owner asked for two police types (tailing chasers; black-and-white interceptors that aim at the player's predicted position, make for upcoming crossings and flank), parked roadblocks from 30 s, red warning chevrons for police arriving from out of sight, and a heat curve in four stages (`HEAT` in `tuning.js`). Parked cruisers don't count toward the police number. Police still spawn in motion, now along a road.
14. **Comic-arcade look (Step 4, 2026-10-30):** owner's own "Arcade Comic Low-Poly" identity: clean low-poly models with comic-book action effects (ink-edged starbursts, puffy cloud smoke, hit-stop). Low sun with shadows about 1.2 x height, falling to the upper left. Palette in `ART_DIRECTION.md`. The owner's PAKO Forever frames are a reference for lighting, shadows and density only: no copied assets, props or branding. Parked civilian cars are breakable props (knocked aside), not physics bodies, to stay cheap on phones.
15. **Crashes and set dressing (2026-10-30):** owner asked for more drama. Wrecks tumble (drawing only: physics stays flat; `render/Tumble.js`), with fireballs, flying wreckage and lasting burns. Word statues are one solid prop per letter, so draw order stays right. Siren beams warn of arriving police. The power-up is an orange gift box, but its arrow and the Shockwave button stay teal, so they are never mistaken for the red warnings. Prop drawings have a memory budget, checked by `tests/test_set.mjs`.
16. **Lucky die (Step 6, 2026-10-30):** the power-up is a violet die, replacing the gift box so the game has its own look (the owner asked for something original). It bounces after the car from the moment it appears, sinks into it, and gives a random item: Shockwave, Giant, Freeze, Pillars or Flamer. Held items fire by themselves after 4 s. Used by double tap (touch), Space / W / Up, gamepad A or the round button. Giant changes the car's real size (`Car.scale`), so collisions, shadows and camera all follow it. Double tap is never the only control (GAME_DESIGN section 9). Audio is on hold: effects reuse existing sounds until the owner's files arrive.
17. **Tactical units (Step 7, 2026-10-30):** owner asked for SWAT Bearcats (from 30 s), roadblocks with spike strips (from 35 s, replacing the 30 s start of decision 13) and a police helicopter (from 45 s). Bearcats are heavy (`BEARCAT`), plough through light props and kill on contact unless the player is Giant; a frozen one can be smashed like any frozen car (decision 18). Spikes give 3 s of flat tires (`FLAT_TIRE`); they only affect the player, and Giant ignores them and mends them. How the car behaves on a flat is in decision 18. The helicopter can't be stopped; it lights up the car and drops concrete blocks ahead, each marked by a red flare 1.2 s first, and a block never lands on the car (`HELICOPTER`). All code-drawn.
18. **Owner's changes after Step 7 (2026-10-30):** (a) The player crashing into a frozen police car smashes it to bits and drives on through, losing 15% of its speed; no wreck, no explosion. This includes frozen Bearcats and parked cruisers. Police hitting a frozen car still both wreck. (b) Power-ups have original names on screen: KABOOM (`shockwave`), TITAN (`giant`), COLD SNAP (`freeze`), STONEWALL (`pillars`), SCORCHER (`flamer`). The code and older notes keep the working names in brackets; `POWERUP.names` holds what the player sees. (c) "Tire" is the spelling everywhere except the owner's copied `PROJECT_INSTRUCTIONS.md`. (d) A flat tire behaves like one failed corner rather than a spin: a jolt, a constant pull to that side that steering can just about hold, speed bleeding off, then a smooth recovery, all within the 3 s. This replaces the "uncontrollable drift spin" of the Step 7 brief.
19. **Weight in the driving (2026-10-31):** owner found the car stiff and asked for realistic turns, with the nose and body tilting. Drawn: the body leans to the outside of a turn as soon as the car turns, overshoots and settles (`chassisRoll`, spring in `Car.moveBody`); the nose dips or lifts (`chassisPitch`) and is drawn slightly ahead of the turn. Drawing only: physics never reads these (tested). The player's car is two sheets, body and wheels (`playerBody`, `playerWheels`); only the wheels are separate, and they share about half the body's movement (`BODY_LEAN.wheelsFollow`), because the owner saw the lower body come apart otherwise. Police lean as one sprite. Physics, on Balanced only, with Grippy and Drifty kept for comparison: the nose comes round more slowly, the tail slides further, and with no steering the nose swings back as the tail catches (`selfAlign`). Top speed now covers forward and sideways together (`slideCountsAsSpeed`, player and police), because cars used to speed up in turns.
20. **Owner's playtest of the above (2026-10-31):** wheels are round on every car (`roundWheel`). A knock or curb makes the player's car jump and land (`CarView.bump`, drawing only). Flying wreckage has a ground shadow, falls faster, and tires bounce and roll on. The on-screen left and right arrows and the white flash when steering are removed, to keep the screen clean. This replaces the "visual feedback" under Controls in `PROJECT_INSTRUCTIONS.md`; the car's own response is the feedback. Hold left or right still steers, and the first-run hint still says so.
21. **Coins, rewarded ad and garage (Step 8, 2026-10-31):** a run pays 2 coins a second, 5 per police car wrecked, 10 per step of its best combo (`COINS`, `coinsForRun`). The results offer to double that by watching a rewarded ad: this replaces "no adverts" in decision 11, and it is the only advert. The reward is given only on Bridge's `rewarded` state, never on `closed` (Playgama's rule). With no platform, or the SDK's `mock` platform, a 2-second countdown stands in for the ad; a platform without rewarded ads shows no button. 19 cars (`CARS`), from free to 100,000 coins: the owner's 5 named cars plus the other kinds in the owner's reference picture, under our own names and our own models. Each has its own handling, size (`scale`, which collisions use) and mass, and heavy ones plow through light props. Bigger cars are always more durable, on the owner's request: durability is the square root of mass, worked out in `tuning.js`, and `tests/test_garage.mjs` checks a bigger car is never less durable. Police speed stays fixed whatever the car, also the owner's choice. Bodies are built from `shell` panels (`lowpoly.js`), and external assets were decided against: the owner agreed code-drawn cars can be improved instead. Too many to keep drawn: only the selected car has drawing sheets, swapped when the car changes; the garage draws one car live on a canvas. Saved through PlatformManager as `totalCoins`, `unlockedCars`, `selectedCar`. A car is selected as soon as it is bought.
22. **Suburban look and UI overhaul (2026-11-01):** owner asked for a new default map and arcade UI, with a set of reference pictures. This replaces the gold-and-yellow islands of decision 14. Palette: slate asphalt, cream curbs, lime grass, white barriers with red stripes. The most common chunk is now `suburb`, and the start chunk always has a gas station in the block ahead (`BUILDERS.station`, at most one a chunk). The canopy roof has no collider and is drawn above the cars (`band: 'overhead'`), so cars drive under it; it and the fuel islands are separate props. Round faceted trees (`faceted` in `lowpoly.js`) replace pines in the layouts. HUD: TIME / DIST / CARS, red PAUSE, RECORD, and a bar toward the next car unlocked by surviving. Results: stars at 10, 20, 35, 50 and 75 s (`STARS`). Garage: a car on a raised block of pavement, white triangle arrows, a lock line and lifetime records. Five cars also unlock by surviving (`surviveSeconds`) and can still be bought, so the approved prices stand. Stat bars stay in the garage, though the brief didn't list them. The references show someone else's game: our names, colours, layouts and models are our own, and no assets were copied.
23. **Maps and identity (2026-11-01):** owner's call, with PAKO screens as reference, and to stay different from them. Four maps (`MAPS`): Suburbs, Winter, Desert, Woods, each its own palette, trees and props. Stars from each map's best run add up and unlock the next (3, 6, 9); that is the only progress there is, shown on the results and map screens. RECORD in play and NEW RECORD on the results are for the map being driven; the garage keeps lifetime records. No progress bar in play, and cars are bought with coins only: this replaces the survival unlocks and the bar of decision 22. Garage: a small, still car on plain road, its name, how to get it, and lifetime records; no stat bars, no pedestal, no turning. The round power-up button is gone: an item goes off by double tap, Space or gamepad A, or by itself after 2.5 s. This replaces "double tap is never the only control", since on touch the automatic firing is the other way to use it. The power-up die is dark amber. One UI colour, amber, on dark ink and white; red stays for danger. All lettering is our own angular strokes (`src/ui/displayFont.js`), the same on every device, with parallelogram buttons.
24. **Better car models and a garage of specs (2026-11-02):** owner gave a picture of four polished low-poly cars as the quality target. Five cars are rebuilt with bodies made of slices (`pressedBody`, `loft` in `lowpoly.js`): Classic Coupe, Pickup, Battering Ram, Rally Wing, Hypercar. The owner's "orange hatchback" is the Hypercar, kept as a wedge; "blue muscle car with a spoiler" is the Rally Wing; "gray SUV" is the Battering Ram; the Pickup was red and is now turquoise with a cream roof. The Coupe was the assistant's suggestion, because every player starts with it. The Hypercar's headlights are gray lenses in dark recesses, not white, on the owner's request. The other 14 cars, the police and the parked cars keep their older models until the owner approves these. In play the body sheet is drawn over the wheel sheet, so these wheels show through real openings in the body; their wheels and a dark floor are drawn first in the garage too. Collision sizes, handling, prices and mass are unchanged. The garage shows each car from behind and above, nose up and to the right, like the owner's PAKO reference (`SHOWN_HEADING`). This hides the front of a car there, so the Ram's orange ram shows only from its top. The car on the title screen no longer turns. In the garage, specs replace the lifetime records and the "no stat bars" of decision 23: SPEED, ACCELERATION, HANDLING, DURABILITY, as ten blocks each, worked out from the real numbers (`carSpecs`). Lifetime records are still saved, but no screen shows them now.
25. **Final visual pass before audio (2026-11-02):** owner's playtest with PAKO frames as reference; the frames show someone else's game and no assets were copied. (a) Map screen: the real map fills the screen, its own ground and props around the start, with your car and a police car on it (`render/mapDiorama.js`, drawn on a plain canvas, so browsing needs no redraw of the world); name and big white arrows over it, stars and status on a dark plate, a locked map dimmed. These files are the one exception to everything being code-made. Files: two music tracks and the effects listed in `AUDIO.files`, in `public/assets`. 29. **Platform rules and clean-up (2026-11-03):** checked against Playgama's game requirements, the Bridge API's required steps and the config page (wiki.playgama.com). (a) Every sound is a file: the owner added bump, cone, UI click, tire squeal and an engine loop, and the code-made sounds the owner left out (explosion, near miss, pickup, start) are gone, with no synthesised fallback. Playgama rejects synthesised sound. The engine is back, from the owner's file, quiet under the music, its pitch rising with speed; this replaces "the engine sound is removed" in decision 26. (b) Esc no longer pauses (the browser uses it); P and the PAUSE button do. (c) On a platform progress is saved only through Bridge storage, and the page-visibility check runs only without a platform or on the SDK's mock (YouTube forbids it; Bridge's pause event covers a hidden page). (d) The SDK (`@playgama/bridge`, installed) is the first script in `<head>`, loaded normally, added by `vite.config.js`; the game reads `platform.language` once and stays English. (e) An interstitial is asked for at the end of every run (`PlatformManager.showInterstitial`, placement `game_over`); Bridge decides whether one is due, the game adds no timer, and it is paused and silent while one shows. This replaces "the only advert" in decision 21. (f) The rewarded button reads WATCH AD: DOUBLE COINS (+n), and COINS DOUBLED! afterwards, without a tick symbol. (g) The debug toolbar and test hooks are left out of `npm run build`; `npm run build:test` keeps them for the browser checks. The old red coupe model and settings nothing read are deleted.

## Platform facts to respect (checked 2026-10-28, re-verify before Milestone 3)

- YouTube Playables: initial bundle MUST be < 30 MiB and SHOULD be < 15 MiB (measured compressed, until `gameReady`).
- No external network calls; all files bundled; relative paths only (`base: './'` in Vite).
- Pause/resume must follow the platform's pause/resume callbacks; platform mute always wins over in-game audio settings.
- Progress must be saved through the platform's save mechanism.
- `gameReady` only when the player can interact (menu or gameplay), never during loading.
- Must work in every aspect ratio, with no orientation lock.
